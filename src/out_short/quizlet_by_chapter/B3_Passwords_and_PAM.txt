Which is FALSE?  (A) You can hash a password you already know and compare  (B) John reverses the hash to get the password back  (C) John the Ripper guesses over and over  (D) Hashes for local users sit in /etc/shadow	John reverses the hash to get the password back  [Hashes are one way. Cracking = guess, hash, compare.]
What is PAM, and who can change it?	Pluggable Authentication Modules - how Linux authenticates you. Admins configure it, and attackers with enough privilege can too.  [Config lives in /etc/pam.d.]
How does pam_exec.so get abused?	It runs any program during login, so an attacker gets a shell on every SSH attempt - even failed ones.
What does pam_pwhistory.so do?	Stops people reusing old passwords.
Read a shadow hash: $6$ vs $1$ vs $y$?	$1$ = MD5, $5$ = SHA-256, $6$ = SHA-512, $y$ = yescrypt. A '!' or '*' means no password login.
Why does a salt matter?	Same password, different hash on every account - so precomputed tables are useless.
